# Assumptions and items needing management approval

## Safety and compliance rules that need sign-off
1. **Monitoring limits.** Monitoring is not limited to temperature: a check measures any parameter (temperature, ppm, pH, minutes) or asks a yes or no, optionally with a batch or lot reference for traceability, and is due at startup, at set times, every N minutes, on chosen weekdays or on a day of the month. Every numeric check has its own minimum and maximum, and a yes or no check passes on Yes. Templates (chiller, freezer, defrosting, cooking, reheating, cooling, hot holding, sanitiser, dishwasher, goods receiving, probe calibration) create a point with starting limits that must be reviewed. The seeded values (wash 55-65 C, final rinse 80-90 C) are placeholders. Set real limits from the manufacturer's instructions and your approved food-safety procedure before use. Only managers can change them, each change is audited, and past readings keep the limits that applied when they were taken.
2. **Machine display versus verification.** A stage is either a machine display reading or an independent probe reading. Model rinse, surface and sanitizing verification as separate stages with their own limits. The system does not decide which standard applies.
3. **Retest timing.** A corrective action schedules a retest after 15 minutes by default; an unresolved failure escalates to the manager after 60 minutes. Both are settings.
4. **Breakage approval.** Incidents of 5 or more units, or an estimated cost of 100 or more, need manager approval. Both thresholds are settings. Lost items are deducted even if stock would go negative, because the loss is real.
5. **Escalation and reminders.** Reminder 30 minutes before a deadline; escalate to the supervisor after 30 minutes overdue and to the manager after 120 minutes. All are settings.

## Behavioural assumptions
- **Late arrival.** Late is minutes after shift start once the shift's grace period is exceeded. The shift is the assigned shift whose start is closest to the check-in time.
- **Payable hours.** Elapsed time minus the shift's break. Overtime is payable time beyond the scheduled shift length, or beyond the shift's own overtime limit if set.
- **Daylight saving.** A wall-clock time that does not exist (spring forward) resolves to the next valid instant; an ambiguous one (fall back) resolves to the first occurrence.
- **Compliance percentages.** Numerator is completed, pending-verification and verified work. Denominator adds failed and overdue work. Waived and future tasks are excluded and reported separately.
- **Stock.** Balances are per item and per location and are derived from an append-only ledger. Opening stock needs a location. There is one item master shared by all locations.
- **Item master.** There is one inventory; categories tell items apart. An item is unique by name ignoring case and spacing, and its ID (`ITM-00001` style) is generated automatically. Items are retired, never deleted. Categories, units, suppliers and dropdown values are retired the same way.
- **Receiving.** A goods receipt adds stock through ledger entries that reference the document and supplier invoice number. A supplier invoice number can be posted once per supplier; a voided document frees it for correct re-entry. Voiding reverses stock with new entries and may take a balance negative if the goods were already used. Updating item costs from an invoice is optional and audited.
- **Suppliers and costs.** Only managers and supervisors can see suppliers, costs and receiving documents; stewards cannot.
- **Permissions.** Supervisors can verify others' work but not their own, and managers cannot approve a transfer or corrective action they raised themselves.
- **Evidence uploads.** JPEG, PNG, WebP and PDF up to 5 MB, checked by file signature. Any signed-in user can fetch an uploaded file if they know its random name.

## Known limits
- The scheduler is in-process (single instance).
- The login lockout is in memory.
- Reports are limited to 93 days per request.
- Email is not implemented. Browser push is: once the keys in DEPLOYMENT.md are set, every new notification is also pushed to the recipient's devices that turned alerts on. It is sent after the notification commits, on a background thread, and a failure never affects the request.
- The backend suite covers business rules and authorization; the frontend has component tests for the main screens.
- **Count limit.** A physical count cannot exceed everything received into that location for the item (opening stock, receipts, transfers in, returns and positive adjustments). Issues and breakage do not lower the limit.
- **Money to a thousandth.** Invoice unit prices, line and invoice discounts, sub totals and totals, and item starting prices are stored and shown to three decimal places (a percentage discount is worked out and kept to three places). The Transfers tab, the Costs tab, the receipts report and the stock cost reports (with their exports) show three places too; Reconciliation, the breakage cost (stored to three places too) and the cost figures in the other reports do as well. The stock value on the inventory pages shows three places too.
- **Receiving discounts.** Line total = quantity x unit cost less the line discount (amount or percent). Sub total = sum of the undiscounted lines. Total = sub total less every line discount and the invoice discount, which applies to the amount left after line discounts. Item cost updates use the net unit price.
- **Week off and absence.** A week off is a shift of its own kind that carries no hours; it cannot overlap a working shift on the same weekday. Week off days are not scheduled and are not chased for check-in. An absence or leave (absent, sick, annual, unpaid, other) is recorded by a manager or supervisor, once per employee and day, and can be cancelled with a reason. Leave is excluded from scheduled work; a recorded absence counts as absent.
- **Role sync.** Roles and their permissions are synchronised to the database on every startup so new permissions reach existing installs.
- **Approvals.** By default every completed checklist task waits for approval (setting `approval.checklists`; set it to 0 to approve only tasks marked for verification). Any supervisor of the location or any manager can approve or reject it; whoever acts first decides, and nobody approves their own work. Monitoring readings are approved the same way, one by one or in bulk from the Monitoring log, and unapproved readings from the last 14 days appear in the pending list. Failed readings also need a corrective action and its approval.
- **Reports.** The Reports page is a library: attendance, checklist completion chart, checklist record, monitoring record, receipts and invoices, stock issues and movements, and the operations summary. Each takes a date range (quick ranges or custom, up to 366 days, 31 for the chart), a location and report-specific filters (employee, checklist frequency, monitoring point, supplier, movement type, item), previews on screen and downloads as PDF, Excel or CSV. The completion chart lists tasks down the page and dates across with a tick per task done, initials of who completed and who approved, and an exceptions table. Every record has a title, period, locations, prepared-by, a table per location, and a signature block, with date and time in the location's time zone. Exports are audited.
- **Manual attendance.** Only a manager can enter a day an employee worked but did not punch (punch in and out, up to 62 days back, never in the future, not when a record or an absence already exists that day). It counts as present, late time is worked out from the shift, the record is marked as a manual entry with the reason, the employee is notified, and the entry is audited. A missed punch-out on an existing record is fixed with Correct.
- **Stock rules.** Stock cannot go negative through issues, returns, transfers or adjustments. A transfer cannot ask for more than the source holds, and is checked again when approved. Breakage is recorded even when the system shows too little stock, because it has physically happened; the shortfall shows as a flagged low stock. Retired items cannot be moved. Item levels, costs and quantities have upper limits, and a target level cannot be below the minimum.
- **Organization and name.** The product is called Plato. The organization name, details and logo come from Settings, Organization.
- **Accounts and passwords.** A manager creates each login (email, role, locations, temporary password). The employee must choose their own password at first sign-in, and again after a manager resets it; until then every other screen and API call is refused. Anyone can change their own password from their name in the header, which needs the current password and ends their other sessions. A manager can reset a forgotten password (a generated temporary password shown once, or one they type), which also ends open sessions, and can correct an email address. Passwords need 10 to 128 characters with at least two kinds of characters and must not contain the sign-in name. Five wrong attempts lock sign-in for that email for 15 minutes. A manager cannot change their own role, reset their own password this way, or deactivate themselves. Reset passwords are never written to the audit log.
- **Log forms.** A manager creates a log form (Monitoring Setup, Create form): a name, a kind of log, the locations that use it, fields and how often it is filled in. A field is a number (with unit, minimum and maximum, failing outside them; a number must have at least one limit), yes or no (a No fails), short text (a maximum length, recorded and never failed) or a dropdown (a list of answers, some of which can be marked as failing). Frequency is every day, certain weekdays, once a month or every few minutes, at one or several times. Each location gets its own copy, each time of day is a separate entry, and all fields of one entry are recorded together; a failing field opens a corrective action like any other check. Answers are required for every field.
- **Search keys and snapshots.** Items carry two stored keys derived from the name so reordered, re-spaced or plural duplicates are refused. Month-end stock balances are stored by the scheduler as a rebuildable copy of the ledger and checked against it every run; the ledger is always the source of truth.

- **Received date and invoice date.** A document has two dates: the day the goods arrived ("Received on", set on the form, today unless changed, never in the future, with no limit on how far back) and the invoice date (it follows Received on until changed by hand). The ledger rows keep the real moment of entry, and the document keeps it too. A manager can correct a posted document's invoice number, both dates and note from Receiving (audited, stock untouched); items, quantities and prices change only by voiding and entering it again. The documents list filters by either date. The cost report counts purchases on the invoice date; stock snapshots and the Insights stock in and out chart stay on the day entered.
- **Invoice dates.** An invoice date more than two years old is refused unless a manager turns on Settings, Receiving, "Allow receiving against invoices older than two years" (off by default); a future date is always refused. The receipts list and the Receipts and invoices report filter by invoice date, and fall back to the day entered when no invoice date was given.
- **Item prices.** The item's own price is a starting price, used until the first priced receipt. The item master also shows the average cost (weighted across the locations the viewer can see) and the last purchase (price after its line discount, with date and supplier, chosen by invoice date). A one-off migration set each item's starting price to its latest priced purchase.
- **Working at several outlets.** A person rostered at one outlet who worked at another that day is shown as "Worked elsewhere" (not absent) at the rostered outlet, and the day is counted once in hours and days-present totals. If they were rostered at both and worked only one, the other is not flagged absent either.
- **Day off from attendance.** A manager with the leave permission can mark a day off from the Attendance list, or change a punch record (within the last 31 days) to a day off; the punch stays in the audit log.
- **Scanning a location code** offers both that location's checklists and its monitoring logs; scanning a monitoring point goes straight to recording its check.
- **Deleting a checklist or task.** Allowed while nothing was done against it. Tasks the scheduler generated that nobody touched, from the last 3 days, do not count as records and are removed with it; anything started, completed, verified or waived, or left untouched for longer than 3 days, blocks deletion and the checklist should be made inactive. A monitoring point with readings can only be made inactive.
- **Templates.** The monitoring templates and the starter checklists are built in; applying one creates ordinary points or checklists that can be edited freely.
- **Training.** A course is an ordered list of lessons: video, audio, document or other attachment, text, a link, or a quiz. Managers build courses under Settings, Training, publish them, and assign them to chosen people, to everyone in a role or to everyone at a location, with an optional due date; only published courses can be assigned and anyone already holding a course keeps their progress. Everyone can take the training assigned to them (the "Take the training assigned to them" permission, which a manager can remove from a role). A lesson is done when marked complete, when a video or audio plays to the end, or when its quiz is passed (quizzes can be retaken; the pass mark is set per course). Where a video or audio was left is remembered and resumed. A course is complete when every lesson is, which records the date, the average quiz result and a certificate number; the certificate is a PDF the person and managers can download. Completed training cannot be taken back. People without a sign-in cannot be assigned. New people are not added to a course automatically when they join a role or location; assign it again. Managers see status, progress, last activity, due and overdue for everyone, and export it to CSV. Overdue reminders are not sent yet; an assignment and a completion each notify the person, including as a push alert.
- **Videos made from slides.** A video lesson can be uploaded or made from up to 40 slides. Each slide has a title, up to 8 points, an optional picture and one of three voice-over choices: none (shown for a set time), typed (read by a computer voice installed on the server) or an uploaded recording (the slide lasts as long as the recording, plus a short pause). A manager can preview each slide, then make the video, which runs in the background and ends up as the lesson's normal file. Changing the slides afterwards marks the video as out of date until it is made again. A video can be at most 30 minutes. Slide text is Latin letters only for now.
